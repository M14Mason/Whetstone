# Keen social playbook — @keen_study_smarter

Written 9 September 2026. Instagram-first, because that is the account that
exists. Everything here assumes one person making content around school, not a
marketing team.

---

## The one thing to get right

Keen has a single idea that no competitor can say: **it works out which units
you keep getting wrong and makes you redo those instead of the stuff you
already know.** Every post should be a different way of saying that. Study-tip
content with the logo on it is what every other study account posts, and it
builds an audience that has no reason to install anything.

The test for any post: *could Quizlet have posted this?* If yes, do not post it.

---

## Realistic cadence

The standard advice for Instagram is one to two posts plus Stories daily. That
is a full-time job and you are in tenth grade. A schedule you abandon in three
weeks is worth less than a smaller one you keep.

| | Volume | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Reels | 3 per week | ~90 min total, batched Sunday |
| Carousel | 1 per week | ~30 min |
| Stories | 3-4 days a week | a few minutes each |
| Comment replies | every day something posts | 10 min |

Batch on Sunday. Post Monday, Wednesday, Thursday, Saturday. Reels between
3pm and 6pm on school days, when the audience is out of class and on their
phones.

---

## Four content pillars

**1. The wrong-answer wedge (40%)**
The differentiator, dramatised. Screen recordings of a weak spot being found,
before/after accuracy on one unit, the reworded-repeat mechanic.

**2. Study science, applied (30%)**
Spaced repetition, the testing effect, why rereading notes fails. Cite the
actual research. This is the pillar that earns saves and shares, which is what
the algorithm rewards, and it makes the product's design look inevitable rather
than arbitrary.

**3. Build in public (20%)**
A sophomore building a study app with 24,322 questions in it. This is a real
advantage: nobody roots for an edtech company, plenty of people root for a
student building one. It also disarms the "who made this" objection.

**4. Course-specific help (10%)**
"The AP Bio unit everyone bombs." Ties directly to the course pages the site
now has, and gives search-driven traffic somewhere to land.

## Hooks that fit this product

Rules: three seconds, text overlay from frame one, no logo intro. Captions on
every video — most people watch without sound, and captions add 25-40% to watch
time.

**Wrong-answer wedge**
1. "You are not bad at chem. You are bad at one unit and you keep re-reading the other five."
2. "I made an app that refuses to show you questions you already get right."
3. "Rereading your notes feels like studying. Here's what it actually does to your recall." *(→ testing effect)*
4. "Watch it find my weak spot in 8 questions."
5. "This is why you get it right in practice and blank in the test." *(→ recognition vs recall)*

**Study science**
6. "Three days before the test is too late. Here is when your brain actually files things."
7. "The highlighter is the single most useless study tool ever invented. Two studies say so."
8. "Studying for four hours and studying four times for one hour are not the same thing."
9. "Your practice test score is inflated. Here is the reason."
10. "Cramming works. For about 48 hours. That is the whole problem."

**Build in public**
11. "I am 15 and I wrote 24,322 practice questions. Here is what broke."
12. "Day 40 of building a study app instead of doing my homework."
13. "I asked 30 people why they hate studying. Same answer 22 times."
14. "The feature I deleted after one week, and why."

**Course-specific**
15. "The AP Bio unit that quietly wrecks everyone's score."
16. "AP Calc: the four question types that are 60% of the exam."
17. "If you keep missing stoichiometry, it is not the maths."

## Reel structures that work here

**Problem → solution, 20-25s.** Hook (0-3s) → the specific pain (3-10s) → the
app doing the thing, screen recorded (10-20s) → "link in bio, free" (20-25s).
Use for pillars 1 and 4.

**Screen recording with narration, 30-45s.** Show the end state first — the weak
spot list — then rewind and show how it got there. Use for the wedge.

**Talking head, 30-60s.** Only for build-in-public. Face on camera is the entire
point of that pillar.

---

## Carousel formats

- **"5 study methods ranked by evidence"** — slide per method, receipts on each. Saves well.
- **"What your wrong answers actually mean"** — misread question vs missing concept vs never learned it.
- **"Every AP course, ranked by how badly people do on unit 3"** — pulled from real bank data once there is usage.

Carousel slide one is a hook, not a title. "5 study methods ranked" is a title.
"Four of these five study methods do nothing" is a hook.

Point the bio link at a course page when a Reel is about a specific class

## Referral loop, on social

The in-app referral gives 14 days each way. Say that out loud roughly once every
two weeks, never more:

> "Send this to whoever you study with. You both get two weeks of everything
> free, and the leaderboard ranks improvement rather than who was already
> ahead — so it is not the class genius winning every week."

Study groups start at three people. Three is the ask, not five, and that is
worth saying explicitly because it is the difference between "I could do that"
and "I would have to organise something".

---

## What to measure

Weekly, five minutes: saves and shares per Reel (not likes), profile visits,
link taps, signups. Saves and shares are the ones that predict reach.

If reach falls: change hooks before anything else. If reach is fine but taps
are not: the video is entertaining but not showing the product. If taps are
fine but signups are not: the landing page is the problem, not the content.

---

## Two honest warnings

**Do not buy followers or engagement.** It poisons the audience signal, and the
account never recovers a real reach curve.

**Do not post other people's study aesthetics as your own.** Reposted notes and
desk photos from Pinterest are copyright problems with a teen-founder story
attached, which is exactly the wrong press.
